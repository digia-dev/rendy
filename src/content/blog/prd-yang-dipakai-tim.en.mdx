Most PRD documents I find fail at one thing: they are written to be presented, not to be used. The format that finally survived in my team has only three parts.

## 1. The problem, in one paragraph

No numbers, no solution. If the problem cannot fit in one paragraph, the problem is not understood yet. This is the paragraph people quote when someone proposes a new feature mid-sprint.

## 2. Decisions already made

This is the section that saves the most time. Every decision is written down with a short reason and the rejected alternative:

- Primary button color: blue — best contrast in light mode.
- No onboarding tour: users arrive from campaigns, not search.

When a debate repeats, the team just reads this section.

## 3. How we'll know it works

Done must be measurable without opening a new discussion. Example: "70% of users complete the checkout flow in a single session, within the first two weeks after release."

Everything else — flows, edge cases, technical specs — goes into the appendix. The appendix can be long; the three main parts cannot.
