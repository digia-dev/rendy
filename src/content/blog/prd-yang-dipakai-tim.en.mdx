Most PRD documents I come across fail at one thing: they are written to be presented, not to be used for real work. The only format that has survived in my team has three parts.

## 1. The problem, in one paragraph

No numbers, no solutions. If the problem cannot be written in one paragraph, it is not understood yet. This is the paragraph that gets quoted when someone offers a new feature in the middle of a sprint.

## 2. Decisions already made

This part saves the most time. Every decision is written down with a short rationale and the alternatives that were rejected:

- Primary button color: blue — best contrast in light mode.
- No onboarding tour: users arrive from campaigns, not from search.

When a debate resurfaces, the team only needs to read this section.

## 3. How we will know it works

The definition of done must be measurable without opening a new discussion. Example: "70% of users complete the checkout flow in a single session, within the first two weeks after release."

Everything else — wireflows, edge cases, technical specs — goes into an appendix. The appendix may be long; the three main parts may not.
