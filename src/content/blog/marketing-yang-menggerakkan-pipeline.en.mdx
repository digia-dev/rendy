Marketing is often measured by volume of activity: campaigns running, content shipping, events held. What rarely gets measured is whether any of it moves the pipeline. This post is about separating the two.

## Start from the pipeline, not the channel

I open every monthly review with one simple question: **how many new opportunities came in, and where did they come from?** If a channel cannot answer that question for two quarters in a row, its budget gets cut without a long debate.

- Log the source of every opportunity in the CRM from day one.
- Review sources at the end of each month, not each year.
- Budget follows proven sources, not visible ones.

## Consistency beats creativity

A big idea that ships once loses to a small routine that ships every week. Audiences build reading habits around schedules, not surprises. In my previous role, steady frequency lifted engagement more than a content rebrand did.

## What I measure every month

1. New opportunities per source.
2. Cost per opportunity (not cost per click).
3. Time from first touch to proposal sent.

Those three numbers are enough to decide where the next quarter gets spent.
