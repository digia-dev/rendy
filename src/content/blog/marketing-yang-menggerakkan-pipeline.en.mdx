Marketing is often measured by the volume of activity: campaigns running, content published, events held. What rarely gets measured is whether that activity actually moves the pipeline. This piece is about telling the two apart.

## Start from the pipeline, not the channel

I always open the monthly review with one simple question: **how many new opportunities came in, and where did they come from?** If a channel cannot answer that question for two quarters in a row, its budget gets cut without a long debate.

- Log the source of every opportunity in the CRM from day one.
- Review sources at the end of every month, not the end of the year.
- Budget follows proven sources, not the most visible ones.

## Consistency beats creativity

A big idea published once loses to a small routine published every week. Audiences build reading habits from schedules, not surprises. In my previous role, keeping the frequency steady lifted engagement far more than a content rebrand.

## What I measure every month

1. New opportunities per source.
2. Cost per opportunity (not cost per click).
3. Time from first contact to the proposal going out.

Those three numbers are enough to decide where the next quarter gets spent.
